Arabic in its UI form, not Noto Sans Arabic itself (2.013): the same
design and project, but 2.013 draws a letter's dots as marks of their
own, which the PDFs' typesetter (Typst 0.15) writes as text runs of
their own, each carrying the letter again, so a PDF's text (copied,
searched, read aloud) would say "شش" for "ش" and "سلام" as "شلام"; the
UI form, whose letters keep their dots and whose height fits a Latin
line, reads back as written.

Unhinted, not hinted: the outlines are the same, the hinting tables
(about 180 KB across the four) serve only Windows' old rasterizer, and
neither the PDFs nor the viewer use them. Kept whole: Arabic and
Devanagari need their shaping tables to join and order their letters.

Emoji are not bundled: a colour emoji font is about 10 MB, so an emoji
in a name prints as an empty box in the PDFs (the viewer draws egui's
own small emoji set).
