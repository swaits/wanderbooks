# Fixed assets

- Register with books.register_asset, inspect with books.asset_schedule and books.list_assets, post monthly depreciation with books.run_depreciation (exempt from the receipt policy; the schedule is the evidence), and dispose with books.dispose_asset (preview first; the bill of sale is the receipt).
- Depreciation is straight-line, book basis only; tax depreciation stays with the CPA.
- It accrues by the day: a partial first month, the final month and a disposal month are prorated by days (the disposal day is held), and the lifetime total is exactly cost minus salvage. A month skipped is caught up by the next run (books.run_depreciation says how much under caught_up; a material miss is a restatement for the CPA); a disposal dated inside a month already run credits the extra days back; one dated before a later month's run is refused until that run is reversed.
- Two dates: the in-service date (in_service_date: the day it is ready and available for its use) starts depreciation; the acquired date is the date of the entry that put it on the books (its purchase or a member's contribution: give it as acquisition_entry, dated on or before the in-service date), and puts it on the register. Something bought before it is used is on the register from the purchase and depreciates from the day it serves.
- Two accumulated figures, never confused: what the books carry (accumulated_posted: its depreciation runs, less any reversed) and what the schedule says is due by a date (accumulated_scheduled_through). Book value is cost less what the books carry; the difference is depreciation not yet run.
- Every asset gets a tag for the label on the thing itself (books.register_asset generates the next one, like FA-0001, unless the human has their own); record the manufacturer's serial number too, change either with books.label_asset, and print label sheets with books.asset_labels (give the human the PDF; print at 100% on Avery 5160/8160). Asset tools take the tag in place of the id.
- Record every asset's tax basis beside its book value (books.set_asset_tax_basis, or basis_rule on books.register_asset): purchase for something bought (basis is its cost); for a member's contribution, carryover_723 (their business property) or personal_use_conversion (their personal property), with the member's adjusted basis (cost plus improvements, less depreciation allowed or allowable) and its fair market value when contributed: ask the human for both and who says so (basis_source), never assume the value is the cost. Three figures follow, each named for what it is: the §723 basis (the member's adjusted basis), the depreciable basis (that basis; for personal use, the lesser of it and the value), and the §704(c) built-in gain or loss (value less §723 basis; a loss counts only for the contributing member). They are at contribution; tax depreciation, and what remains of the gain or loss later, are the CPA's. Record the §704(c) method (traditional, traditional_with_curative, remedial) once chosen with the CPA. Book value stays fair market value.
- books.export fixed_asset_register is the register for the CPA (posted and scheduled, every tax figure, reconciled to the books); the CPA package's k1-tax-basis-capital.csv carries each member's capital on the tax basis beside the book figures, with what only the CPA can supply left open. books.show asset opens one in the viewer.
