# K-U10: `Decimal`'s sums and products are exact or refused (`kernel/decimal_exact.rs`)

_Folded into `docs/verification.md` by the integrator; this note is the
unit's own record._

Plan unit K-U10a (`plan/p14-bookspec.md`, the K-U10 row). Rules: BS-ledger-1
(a product the arithmetic cannot hold MUST be refused; a foreign amount is
booked as the exact product rounded once) and BS-entry-4 (what a line can
carry). The tie-ranking item of the K-U10 row (`split_by_weights_into`'s
exact remainders) is an owner decision and is **not** in this unit.

## The findings

`rust_decimal`'s `checked_add` and `checked_mul` return `None` only when
the integer part overflows. When the exact result needs more than 96 bits
of mantissa at the operands' scale, or a scale past 28, they return a
**rounded** `Some`:

| Operation | Exact | `rust_decimal` | Found by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MAX + 0.4` | 79228162514264337593543950335.4 | `Some(MAX)` | K-U4 |
| `0.01 + 792281625142643375935439503.35` | …503.36 | `…503.4` | K-U8 |
| `1050000010.0000000001 x 9.9999999999` | 10500000099.89499999999999999999 | `10500000099.895000000000000000` | K-U1 |
| `123456789.0123456789²` | 15241578753238836.75019051998750190521 | `15241578753238836.750190519988` | K-U3 |
| `1e-28 x 1e-28` | 1e-56 | `Some(0)` | K-U1 |

`checked_div` (rounds at 28 significant digits) is the engine's rounding
division and is unchanged, with `checked_div_exact`.

## The method

`impl Amount for Decimal` (`kernel/entries.rs`) runs `rust_decimal`'s
operation and then checks, in 256-bit integers, that the figure it returned
**equals the exact result as a rational**; if not, `None`. It never builds a
figure of its own, so:

1. `Some(r)` is the exact sum or product (checked, not assumed).
2. A result that was exact is returned **as `rust_decimal` gave it, scale
   and all**: nothing exact moves, by construction.
3. A result that was rounded is `None`; one that was `None` stays `None`.

The check (`decimal_exact.rs`): a `Decimal` is `m x 10^-s`, `|m| < 2^96`,
`s <= 28`. For a sum `a + b = r`, at the common scale `s = max(sa, sb, sr)`
every term `m x 10^(s-si)` is under `2^96 x 10^28 < 2^190`, so the equation
is `Σ positive terms == Σ negative terms` over unsigned 256-bit values,
with no overflow possible. For a product, `ma x mb` (under `2^192`, at
scale `sa + sb <= 56`) is compared with `mr` at scale `sr` by scaling the
side with fewer places up; overflow past 256 bits means that side is the
larger, so unequal. Zero and sign are handled first. Any failure to compute
answers "not exact".

**Why not "refuse a sum whose result scale fell below the operands'".** The
K-U10 row's test is sound for a rounding but not complete as an exactness
test in the other direction: it would refuse exact figures (a product whose
full-scale mantissa passes 96 bits but whose last digits are zeros, like
`100 x 0.3333333333333333333333333333` = 33.33…3300, which `percent_of`
computes for every ratio, or `10^20 x 10^-20` squared) and `periods.rs`'s
hand-written scale check does exactly that. Comparing values is the
complete test, so nothing exact is refused (`an_exact_product_that_only_looks_wide_is_kept`).

`Amount::checked_sub` for `Decimal` is `rust_decimal`'s subtraction under
the same check. The trait default (`self + (-rhs)`) gave `-0` for `0 - 0`;
this keeps the old inherent subtraction's `0`, which is also what
`Money::checked_sub` had (`Money::checked_add` and `checked_sub` now go
through `Amount`, so they inherit the fix).

## Two products that must keep rounding or round once

Making `checked_mul` exact would have refused posted-range inputs, found by
running the suite (`spec_cash_flow`:
`a_shared_government_payment_splits_to_the_cent`):

- **Shares** (`share_of`, `share_floor_with`, so `split_by_weights_with`)
  multiply a cent amount by a percent of 27 places
  (`33.333333333333333333333333333`), whose exact product needs 29 places;
  then divide, which rounds anyway. They use a new
  `Amount::checked_mul_rounded` (`rust_decimal`'s own product; on integers
  `checked_mul`), so every share is the figure it always was, to the digit.
- **A foreign amount at a rate** (`fx_booked`) is not rounded early:
  `Amount::checked_mul_round_to_step` holds the exact product in 256 bits,
  cuts it only as far as `Decimal` needs while remembering whether any cut
  digit was non-zero (a sticky digit, kept below at least one real digit
  past the step), and rounds **once** with `round_step`. The sticky digit
  decides a tie (a half followed by zeros) and the directed modes as the
  full product would. Consequence: `1050000010.0000000001 x 9.9999999999`
  books **10500000099.89**, the exact product's cent, where it booked
  10500000099.90 through a rounded 10500000099.895. This is the one posted
  behaviour that changes (the D-75 ten-place bound alone does not make the
  product fit 96 bits, past about 7.9e8). `None` only for a figure too large
  to hold with its rounding digits (about 7.9e24 at a cent, 10^9 times a
  journal line's cap).

Everywhere else a product is the amount itself (`line_amount`,
`deductible_amount`, `percent_of`, the gold/no-minor-unit FX product) and
uses `checked_mul`: exact or `None`, each caller turning `None` into its
existing refusal.

## Kani

None added. Kani works on `i64`, which never rounds; `Decimal` is out of its
reach (one symbolic `Decimal` add runs for more than ten minutes), so the
claim rests on the bridge properties below and on Lean's laws over exact
rationals, which these results now equal. The traceability gate's
`EXCEPTIONS` is unchanged: the new items are `pub(super)` helpers and trait
methods, not kernel functions.

## Bridge properties (`tests/kernel_bridge/decimal_exact.rs`)

Compared against `rust_decimal`'s own result (the old behaviour) and an
independent `num_rational::BigRational` reference, over every mantissa to
`2^96 - 1` (small, near the top, trailing zeros) and every scale 0 to 28, at
30,000 cases each:

| Property | Law |
|---|---|
| `add_is_exact_or_none` | `Some(r)` equals the rational sum; equals the old result to the character where that was exact; `None` where it was rounded or `None` |
| `sub_is_exact_or_none` | the same, against `rust_decimal`'s subtraction (zero included) |
| `mul_is_exact_or_none` | the same, for products |
| `a_refusal_is_an_overflow_or_a_rounding` | the kernel refuses only where `rust_decimal` overflowed or rounded |
| `posted_range_sums_are_unchanged` | every line-range figure (≤ 10^15, ≤ 10 places, any raw scale holding it): add and subtract equal the old to the character and are never `None` |
| `posted_range_running_totals_are_unchanged` | a running total of up to 100 line-range figures is unchanged at every step |
| `posted_range_fx_products_are_unchanged_or_refused` | foreign (≤ 10^25 mantissa, ≤ 10 places) x rate (≤ 10 places): identical to the old whenever the mantissa product fits 96 bits; otherwise the old figure only if it was exact, else `None` |
| `the_fx_product_is_rounded_once_from_the_exact_one` | `checked_mul_round_to_step` equals the exact rational product rounded once, in each of four modes, at steps 1, 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001, signs and ties included; `None` only past about 10^23 |

Also: the three flipped canaries (below), `an_exact_product_that_only_looks_wide_is_kept`,
`zero_is_exact`, `the_k_u1_edge_books_the_cent_the_exact_products_cent`
(and, in `fx.rs`, `the_mantissa_edge_books_the_exact_products_cent`), and
four unit tests inside `decimal_exact.rs` that check the private 256-bit
helpers (`mul_wide`, `checked_add`, `checked_mul_small`, `div_rem_10`,
`scale_up`) against `num_bigint` with edge values.

## What did not change

- `checked_div`, `checked_div_exact` (still a rounded product inside
  `checked_div_exact`: left alone, division is not this unit's).
- Every golden and every existing test outside the list above.
- Direct calls of the inherent `Decimal::checked_add/_sub/_mul` outside the
  kernel (about 70 sites in `reports`, `tax_capital`, `assets`, `arap`,
  `periods`, `schedules`, …) still round at the 96-bit edge: not converted,
  because `periods.rs` shares by a many-place percent, for one, relies on a
  rounded product, and each is unreachable below about 7.9e26. A follow-up
  can convert them one by one against its own refusal.
