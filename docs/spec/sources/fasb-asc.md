# FASB Accounting Standards Codification: paragraphs read directly

Read 2026-10-14 in the ASC's basic view (asc.fasb.org, Stephen's login).
The Codification is copyrighted by the Financial Accounting Foundation,
so this file keeps only short quotes and paraphrase, with the paragraph
numbers to look up. Earlier ASC paragraphs were read through Deloitte's
DART roadmaps (see README).

## 360-10-35-4 (Property, plant, and equipment: depreciation)

A productive facility's cost is spread over its expected useful life,
allocated "as equitably as possible" to the periods that get its
services. Depreciation distributes cost, "less salvage (if any)", over
the estimated useful life "in a systematic and rational manner"; it is
"a process of allocation, not of valuation." No method is prescribed.
→ B-15: straight line, salvage never depreciated, partial months by
days (each day of service bears the same cost) is one systematic and
rational allocation.

## 340-10-05-2, -05-4, -05-5 (Other assets and deferred costs: prepaids)

Prepaid expenses are assets "typically used up or expire within the
normal operating cycle," paid "in advance of their use or consumption"
(05-4); examples include insurance, interest, rents, taxes, and
advertising not yet received (05-5). The Subtopic covers only their
nature (05-2): it prescribes no expensing method.
→ B-16: a prepaid is an asset, expensed over the months it covers
(a convention; the ASC is silent on the method).

## 210-10-45-1, -45-2, -45-5 (Balance sheet: current assets and liabilities)

Prepaid expenses are among current assets (45-1(g)), not because they
become cash but because they would otherwise use current assets in the
operating cycle (45-2). A total of current liabilities "shall be
presented in classified balance sheets" (45-5): the rule applies when a
balance sheet is classified.
→ wanderbooks' balance sheet is unclassified (by element), which this
Subtopic permits; a classified layout would need current/noncurrent
account roles.

## 230-10-45-7, -10, -12(a), -13(a), -14(b), -15(b) (Statement of cash flows)

Gross amounts are "more relevant" than net (45-7). Cash receipts and
payments are classified "as resulting from investing, financing, or
operating activities" (45-10). Collections on, and disbursements for,
loans the entity makes are investing (45-12(a), 45-13(a)). Proceeds of
"other short- or long-term borrowing" are financing inflows (45-14(b));
"repayments of amounts borrowed" are financing outflows (45-15(b)).
→ B-14: money owners advance (Due to Shareholders) is borrowing, so
financing, gross; a loan the company makes is investing, gross.

## 606-10-55-36 through 55-38 (Revenue: principal or agent)

An entity controlling the specified good or service before transfer is
the principal (55-37) and recognizes revenue "in the gross amount of
consideration" it expects (55-37B); an agent recognizes only its fee or
commission (55-38).
→ B-17: an app seller is the principal for its app, so its revenue is
the gross price; the platform's commission is an expense.
