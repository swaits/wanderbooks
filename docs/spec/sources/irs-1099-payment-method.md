**(e)(1)**

> (e) Payment made on behalf of another person—(1) In general. A person that makes a payment in the course of its trade or business on behalf of another person is the payor that must make a return of information under this section with respect to that payment if the payment is described in paragraph (a) of this section and, under all the facts and circumstances, that person—
>
> (i) Performs management or oversight functions in connection with the payment (this would exclude, for example, a person who performs mere administrative or ministerial functions such as writing checks at another's direction); or
>

**(e)(2)**

>
> (B) A network of persons unrelated to each other, and to the issuer, who agree to accept such cards as payment; and
>

The use of a “payment card” as defined in paragraph (b)(3) of this section by a cardholder to withdraw funds at an automated teller machine, or to obtain a cash advance or loan against the cardholder's account, is not a payment card transaction under paragraph (b)(1) of this section because the card is not being accepted as payment by a merchant or other payee.
>

The acceptance of a check issued in connection with a payment card account by a merchant or other payee is not a payment card transaction under paragraph (b)(1) of this section because the check is accepted and processed through the banking system in the same manner as a traditional check, not as a payment card.

**(c)(1)**

> (c) Third party network transactions—(1) Definition. The term third party network transaction means any transaction that is settled through a third party payment network.

**(c)(2)**

> (2) Third party settlement organization. The term third party settlement organization means the central organization that has the contractual obligation to make payments to participating payees (as defined in paragraph (a)(5)(i)(B) of this section) of third party network transactions. A central organization is a third party settlement organization if it provides a third party payment network (as defined in paragraph (c)(3)(i) of this section) that enables purchasers to transfer funds to providers of goods and services.

**(c)(3)**

>
> (C) Guarantees payment to the persons providing goods or services in settlement of transactions with purchasers pursuant to the agreement or arrangement.
>
> (ii) A third party payment network does not include any agreement or arrangement that provides for the issuance of payment cards.
>
> (iii) Persons who are providers of goods and services as described in this paragraph (c)(3) are participating payees within the meaning of paragraph (a)(5)(i)(B) of this section.

**Specific Instructions: payment settlement entity, and Note**

> A PSE is a domestic or foreign entity that is a merchant acquiring entity, that is, a bank or other organization that has the contractual obligation to make payment to participating payees in settlement of payment card transactions; or a TPSO, that is, the central organization that has the contractual obligation to make payments to participating payees of third party network transactions.
>
> Healthcare networks, in-house accounts payable departments, and automated clearing houses do not qualify as TPSOs and do not report under section 6050W.

**Specific Instructions: participating payee**

> A participating payee is any person, including any governmental unit (and any agency or instrumentality of a governmental unit) who accepts a payment card, or any account number or other indicia associated with a payment card, as payment or accepts payment from a TPSO in settlement of a third party network transaction.

**Specific Instructions: payment card**

> A payment card is any card, including any stored-value card (having prepaid value, including gift cards), issued according to an agreement or arrangement that provides for all of the following.
>
> One or more issuers of the cards.
>
> A network of persons unrelated to each other, and to the issuer, who agree to accept the cards as payment.
>
> Standards and mechanisms for settling the transactions between the merchant acquiring entities and the persons who agree to accept the cards as payment.

**Specific Instructions: third party payment network**

> A third party payment network is any agreement or arrangement that provides for the following.
>
> The establishment of accounts with a central organization by a substantial number of providers of goods or services who are unrelated to the organization and who have agreed to settle transactions for the provision of the goods or services to purchasers according to the terms of the agreement or arrangement.
>
> Standards and mechanisms for settling the transactions.
>
> Guarantee of payment to the persons providing goods or services (participating payees) in settlement of transactions with purchasers pursuant to the agreement or arrangement.
>
> A third party payment network does not include any agreement or arrangement that provides for the issuance of payment cards.

**Reporting under sections 6041, 6041A, and 6050W**

> Payments made by payment card or through a third party payment network after December 31, 2010, that otherwise would be reportable under sections 6041 (payments made in the course of a trade or business to another person) or 6041A(a) (payments of remuneration for services and certain direct sales) and 6050W are reported under section 6050W and not section 6041 or 6041A. For purposes of determining whether payments are subject to reporting under section 6050W, rather than section 6041 or 6041A, the de minimis threshold, discussed later under Box 1a, is disregarded.

**Introduction**

> Form 1099-K is a report of payments you received for goods or services during the year from:
>
> Credit, debit or stored value cards such as gift cards (payment cards)
>
> Payment apps or online marketplaces, also called third party settlement organizations or TPSOs

**Who gets Form 1099-K**

> If your customers or clients pay you directly by credit, debit or gift card, you'll get a Form 1099-K from your payment card processor no matter how many payments you got or how much they were for.

**Personal payments from family and friends**

> Money you received from friends and family as a gift or repayment for a personal expense should not be reported on a Form 1099-K. These payments aren't taxable income.
>
> Be sure to note these types of payments as non-business in the payment apps when possible.

**Form 1099-MISC: Reportable payments to corporations**

> The following payments made to corporations must generally be reported on Form 1099-MISC.
>
> Cash payments for the purchase of fish for resale reported in box 11.
>
> Medical and health care payments reported in box 6.
>
> Substitute payments in lieu of dividends or tax-exempt interest reported in box 8.
>
> Gross proceeds paid to an attorney reported inbox 10.

**Form 1099-MISC, Box 6: Medical and Health Care Payments (second paragraph)**

> The exemption from issuing Form 1099-MISC to a corporation does not apply to payments for medical or health care services provided by corporations, including professional corporations. However, you are not required to report payments made to a tax-exempt hospital or extended care facility or to a hospital or extended care facility owned and operated by the United States (or its territories), a state, the District of Columbia, or any of their political subdivisions, agencies, or instrumentalities.

**Form 1099-NEC: Reportable payments to corporations**

> The following payments made to corporations must generally be reported on Form 1099-NEC.
>
> Attorneys’ fees reported inbox 1a.
>
> Payments by a federal executive agency for services (vendors) reported inbox 1a.

**Form 1099-NEC: Payments to corporations for legal services**

> The exemption from reporting payments made to corporations does not apply to payments for legal services. Therefore, you must report attorneys’ fees (in box 1a of Form 1099-NEC) or gross proceeds (in box 10 of Form 1099-MISC) as described earlier to corporations that provide legal services.

**Form 1099-NEC: Payments made on behalf of another person**

> For payments reportable under section 6041, if you make a payment on behalf of another person who is the source of the funds, you may be responsible for filing Form 1099-NEC. You are the payor for information reporting purposes if you perform management or oversight functions in connection with the payment, or have a significant economic interest in the payment (such as a lien). For example, a bank that provides financing to a real estate developer for a construction project maintains an account from which it makes payments for services in connection with the project. The bank performs management and oversight functions over the payments and is responsible for filing information returns for payments of $2,000 or more paid to contractors. For more information, see Regulations section 1.6041-1(e).

**Form 1099-NEC: Exceptions**

> Some payments do not have to be reported on Form 1099-NEC, although they may be taxable to the recipient. Payments for which a Form 1099-NEC is not required include all of the following.
>
> Generally, payments to a corporation (including an LLC) that is treated as a C or S corporation). However, see Reportable payments to corporations , earlier.
>
> Payments for merchandise, telegrams, telephone, freight, storage, and similar items.
>
> Payments of rent to real estate agents or property managers. However, the real estate agent or property manager must use Form 1099-MISC to report the rent paid over to the property owner. See Regulations section 1.6041-3(d); Regulations section 1.6041-1(e)(5), Example 5; and, the instructions for box 1a.

> Wages, any bonuses, prizes, and awards paid to employees (report these on Form W-2).

## Derived (wanderbooks)

- **Cards and networks go on 1099-K.** A payment made by payment card or through a third party payment network that §6041 would otherwise cover is reported under §6050W on Form 1099-K, by the payment settlement entity, and not under §6041, so it does not go on Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC (§1.6041-1(a)(1)(iv); the 1099-K instructions extend this to §6041A). For this test the third party network de minimis of §1.6050W-1(c)(4) (more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions) is disregarded, so the exclusion holds below it. A payment card is any card, including a stored-value card, meaning any card with a prepaid value, gift cards included (§1.6050W-1(b)(3)(i), (b)(4)). The regulation does not name credit or debit cards; the IRS page names credit, debit and stored-value cards as payment cards. The rule covers payments made after December 31, 2010.
- **The card must be accepted by the payee.** A payment card transaction is one in which the card, or its number or other indicia, is accepted as payment (§1.6050W-1(b)(1)); the participating payee is the person who accepts the card as payment ((a)(5)(i)(A), (b)(3)(ii)). The regulation's own exclusions turn on that test: an ATM withdrawal or cash advance is not a payment card transaction because the card is not being accepted as payment by a merchant or other payee ((b)(5)(i)), and neither is a convenience check, which is processed through the banking system ((b)(5)(ii)). So a card that only funds a transfer which the payee does not accept as a card payment is not a payment card transaction with that payee. The texts do not name card-funded transfers; this rests on (b)(1), (a)(5)(i)(A) and (b)(3)(ii), with (b)(5) as the regulation's examples. If the payee instead accepts payment from a third party settlement organization, the transaction is a third party network transaction ((a)(5)(i)(B), (c)(1) to (c)(3)).
- **ACH is not a TPSO.** Automated clearing houses do not qualify as third party settlement organizations and do not report under §6050W (1099-K instructions, Note). A plain ACH transfer (no card, no TPSO) is therefore not a §6050W transaction, and §1.6041-1(a)(1)(iv) does not take it off Form 1099-NEC/MISC.
- **Not reportable under §6041** (§1.6041-3, introductory text): payments of bills for merchandise, telegrams, telephone, freight, storage, and similar charges ((c)); and payments to:
  - a corporation ((p)(1)), except attorneys' fees paid after December 31, 1997 and a corporation providing, or billing and collecting for, medical and health care services; even then nothing is reported for a §501(c)(3) tax-exempt hospital or extended care facility, or one owned and operated by the United States, a State, the District of Columbia, a possession, or a political subdivision, agency or instrumentality of any of them;
  - an organization exempt from tax under §501(a), or an individual retirement plan ((p)(2));
  - the United States ((p)(3));
  - a State, the District of Columbia, a possession of the United States, or a political subdivision of any of them ((p)(4));
  - a foreign government or a political subdivision of one ((p)(5));
  - a foreign payee or foreign beneficial owner, when the payor holds documentation it may rely on before paying (§1.6041-4(a)(1)).

  Wages paid to an employee go on Form W-2 (§1.6041-2(a)(1)). The 1099-MISC/NEC instructions count an LLC treated as a C or S corporation as a corporation, and list payments to corporations that stay reportable: cash for fish bought for resale (1099-MISC box 11), medical and health care payments (box 6), substitute payments in lieu of dividends or tax-exempt interest (box 8), gross proceeds paid to an attorney (box 10), attorneys' fees (1099-NEC box 1a), and payments by a federal executive agency for services (1099-NEC box 1a).
- **Treating a payee as a corporation.** Absent actual knowledge otherwise, a payor may treat a payee as a corporation if, before payment, its name carries Incorporated, Inc., Corporation, Corp., or P.C. (Company or Co. does not count), or the payor has a corporate resolution or similar document on file (a copy of a Form 8832 association election counts), or a Form W-9 with an EIN stating it is a domestic corporation, or a foreign-corporation withholding certificate (§1.6049-4(c)(1)(ii)(A)(1) to (4)).
- **The payor.** The payor is the person engaged in a trade or business who makes the payment in the course of it (§1.6041-1(a)(1)(i)). A person who pays on behalf of another is the payor only if it performs management or oversight functions in connection with the payment or has a significant economic interest in it; mere administrative or ministerial functions, such as writing checks at another's direction, are not management or oversight (§1.6041-1(e)(1)(i), (ii)). If two or more qualify, the one closest in the chain to the payee reports unless they agree in writing otherwise ((e)(2)). The quoted texts do not name a member paying the company's bill from personal funds; under (e)(1) a member who only makes the payment at the company's direction is not the payor.
- **Corporate status by election.** An entity organized under a Federal or State statute that describes it as incorporated or as a corporation is a corporation, and so is an association as determined under §301.7701-3 (§301.7701-2(b)(1), (2); see `irs-entity-classification.md`). An election on Form 8832 takes effect on the date the form specifies, or on the date filed if it specifies none; the date can be no more than 75 days before nor more than 12 months after filing, and a date outside that range is moved to the limit (§301.7701-3(c)(1)(iii)).
- **What §70433 changed.** It raised the §6041(a) amount from $600 to $2,000 ((a)), indexed it for calendar years after 2026 in a new §6041(h), each increase rounded to the nearest $100 ((b)), made §6041A(a)(2)'s nonemployee-compensation threshold the §6041(a) amount in effect for the calendar year ((c)) and §3406(b)(6)'s backup-withholding amount the same ((d)), and made §6041(a) count by calendar year ((e)(2)). So the $2,000 holds for Form 1099-NEC (§6041A(a)(2)) and for the 1099-MISC payments reported under §6041(a) (its "rent, salaries, wages, premiums, annuities, compensations, remunerations, emoluments, or other fixed or determinable gains, profits, and income": `irs-1099-thresholds.md`) alike. It changed neither §6050N(a)'s $10 for royalties nor §1.6045-5(a)(1)'s $600 for payments to an attorney in connection with legal services (gross proceeds, 1099-MISC box 10), which leaves out what §6041(a) already reports, or would but for its dollar amount ((c)(4)): attorneys' fees for the payor's own legal work are §6041's, at its amount. The texts quoted here don't say whether payments a payee gets on both forms add up toward one threshold.
- **Dates.** §1.6041-1(a)(1)(iv) governs payments made after December 31, 2010. The amendments of Pub. L. 119-21 §70433 (the $2,000 threshold, `irs-1099-thresholds.md`) apply to payments made after December 31, 2025 (§70433(f)).
